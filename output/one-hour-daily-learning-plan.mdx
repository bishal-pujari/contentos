## The 1-Hour Daily Learning Plan

A lot of people ask how they can learn something like cloud or data science while working full time. When you look at the syllabus, it feels overwhelming. There’s always more to cover, and it’s not clear where to begin.

But here’s what I’ve found. You don’t need to quit your job or study for five hours every evening. What you need is a simple system that you can repeat consistently without burning out.

This is a one-hour daily plan that works well because it keeps things focused and manageable.

---

## The Problem With Traditional Studying

Most people start by opening a long course and trying to push through it after work. It feels productive in the beginning, but after some time, focus drops. You’re tired, and the information becomes harder to absorb.

The issue isn’t effort. It’s that the approach is too passive. Watching and reading alone doesn’t create strong understanding, especially when your energy is limited.

---

## The 60-Minute Daily Loop

Instead of trying to do everything at once, break your learning into three small parts. Each part has a purpose, and together they help you stay consistent.

---

### 1. Learn With Focus (20 Minutes)

Start with one small topic. Not an entire module—just one concept.

For example, you might spend a session understanding virtual networks, IAM roles, or how a specific service works. Use documentation, short videos, or even AI tools to clarify concepts.

The goal here is not depth. It’s familiarity. You should be able to recognize the concept and explain it in simple terms.

### 2. Test Yourself (30 Minutes)

This is where learning becomes real.

After going through a topic, you need to check whether you actually understood it. Not by rereading, but by answering questions and working through scenarios.

This is where a tool like ExamOS fits naturally. You can use this block to take a focused 30-minute quiz and see how well you grasp the topic.

At first, you’ll get things wrong. That’s expected. This is how you identify gaps.

### 3. Review and Reflect (10 Minutes)

Most people skip this step, but it’s where the real progress happens.

Instead of just checking your score, spend a few minutes understanding your mistakes. Look at the questions you got wrong and read the explanations carefully.

Try to notice patterns. Are you confused about networking? Are you mixing up similar services?

These patterns tell you exactly what to focus on next.

---

## Why This Works

This approach removes passive learning. You’re not just consuming content—you’re actively engaging with it.

You learn a small concept, test your understanding, and then fix your mistakes. That loop, repeated daily, builds real confidence over time.

## Final Advice

You don’t need a perfect plan. You just need one that you can follow regularly.

If you can give one focused hour a day, five days a week, you’ll start seeing progress sooner than you expect. Keep it simple, stay consistent, and let the progress build over time.
